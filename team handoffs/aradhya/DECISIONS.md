## D-2026-11-13-GLASS — Premium glass layer (user-directed amendment of the design standard)
**Instruction.** The product owner asked for a markedly more premium interface: good design and colour, glassy backgrounds, nothing that reads as generic AI output. Architecture 41 forbade glassmorphism (X-03), gradients (X-02), elevation shadows and halos (X-04) and radii above 6 px (X-05), so the standard and its gates were amended. Nothing outside the stated scope was relaxed.
**What changed.** Architecture 41 §5.5 (new), the four rows above, VIS-03, VIS-19 and VIS-20. `apps/web/src/styles/glass.css` is the only stylesheet that may hold backdrop blur, gradients, radii above 6 px and elevation shadows. Its permitted surfaces are listed once in `apps/web/design/glass-surfaces.json`. One new element, `.ambient-canvas`, renders a static backdrop of blue, sky and teal radial fields (no violet, no animation, no space imagery).
**Why this stays safe.**
- Glass frames data; it never sits behind it. Tables, charts, maps, histograms, route plots and inputs stay opaque.
- `forbidden_scan.py` exempts `glass.css` from exactly two rules (decorative effect, large radius) and validates it structurally against the contract. `tests/contracts/ui/test_glass_contract.py` damages it in 18 ways and removes each of four fallbacks; the scanner must fail every time. Every other stylesheet is still scanned in full, and a test proves that.
- Audit C10 admits backdrop, gradient, shadow and radius only on contract selectors and within its limits. No threshold changed (contrast 4.5 and 3, CLS 0.02, radius 6, target sizes all as before).
- **Audit contrast on glass is stricter, not weaker.** The existing check walks to the first opaque ancestor, so a translucent panel would have been judged against the canvas colour and reality would be invisible. Text backed by glass is now measured against the painted background: the audit renders a second frame with all glyphs transparent and samples every text box (5 by 3 grid, worst sample wins). A first attempt that sampled a ring around the text box reported false failures from neighbouring glyphs on the same line (for example a separator against the next word); it was replaced, not tuned.
- The audit's `rgb()` helper treated `color(srgb 0.07 0.09 0.12 / 0.7)`, the form Chrome serialises for `color-mix()`, as 0-255 values. It now scales it correctly. This is a bug fix that the glass checks depend on.
- Fallbacks are required by the scanner: no `backdrop-filter` support, `prefers-reduced-transparency`, `forced-colors` and print all restore flat, opaque, ink-free surfaces.
**Findings during development, all fixed rather than excused.** (1) A 100 ms colour transition on buttons made the audit measure a button mid-transition (a foreground that is not a token). The base layer sets `.button { transition: none }` for exactly this reason, so the transition was removed and the rule recorded. (2) `.planetary-container` had been wrongly classed as an opaque frame although `planetary-panel` is also a glass panel; the audit rejected the blur and the contract was corrected. (3) `.drawer-right` and `.drawer-bottom` are drawers and were added to chrome.
**Not done on purpose.** No neon, glow, coloured shadow, starfield, hero or marketing treatment (X-04, X-06, X-07 stand). No palette value was added or changed; `tokens.json` and `tokens.css` are untouched.
**Findings from the full 501-check catalog, all fixed rather than excused (D-2026-11-13-GLASS, continued).**
- *Occluded and off-screen text.* At 390 px the audit scrolls content behind the sticky header. With an opaque header that text was invisible and harmless; with glass it appeared in the glyph-free frame as header pixels. The glass contrast check now measures only text that is actually exposed (the topmost element at the text box centre is the text's own element or related to it); occluded text cannot be read and is not measured.
- *Canvas-overlay boundary.* The existing test for text over a map used the first opaque ancestor as its backing. With translucent glass that ancestor became the root element, which contains every canvas, producing false positives for header text. The boundary is now the nearest glass or opaque ancestor.
- *Computed colour versus painted pixels.* Text backed by translucent glass is no longer also judged by the computed-colour walk, which cannot see through translucency and compared nested-theme text with the wrong page canvas. The painted-pixel measurement is the authority for it. An opaque glass-listed element (reduced-transparency rendering) counts as a solid backing and keeps the computed check.
- *Nested theme scopes.* The style guide renders a dark section inside a light page and the reverse. Custom properties that reference other variables are resolved where they are declared, so root-level glass variables gave nested sections the page theme's colours (light text on light glass, contrast 1.1). Theme-dependent glass variables are now declared on every themed element, as tokens.css does; a nested theme scope paints its own canvas; and the scanner enforces the first rule (`glass-theme-scope`, 19 damaged-stylesheet tests).
- *Environment.* The mock passport renderer is a Vite middleware that spawns `.venv/bin/python`. A worktree without that virtualenv returns 503 `MOCK_RENDERER_UNAVAILABLE`, the mock run cannot finish, and C12 reports it. This was a setup error and not a product defect; the RC control run, with the venv, passed the unmodified catalog.
- *Capture size budget.* The component-guide PNG budget (400 KB per part) is unchanged. One dark capture grew from 289 KB to 414 KB because the ambient gradient's dithering covers a very large area in a full-page capture (measured: 301 KB without it, and the blur is not the cause). The committed component captures omit the ambient layer. It is present in every audited state and every S01 to S12 capture.
