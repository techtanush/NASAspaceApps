Gale Crater and Flagstaff

Where on Earth could a rover team rehearse driving on terrain like the area we studied on Mars? We used NASA elevation data to compare four areas in Arizona with our Mars target.

Gale Crater, Mars
A large impact crater where NASA’s Curiosity rover landed in 2012. We used a region within the crater as the terrain reference. NASA’s guide to Gale Crater
Flagstaff, Arizona, Earth
A city in northern Arizona, USA. The four boxes on the map are areas north of the city that we screened as possible places to scout for rover tests.

Two of four areas met the terrain threshold

We compared the mix of flat ground, gentle slopes, and steep hills in each area. A smaller score means the overall mix is closer to the Mars target.

Our cutoff was a difference of 5° or less. The score describes the whole area. Individual hills can be much steeper. Both elevation maps were compared at the same 500 m scale.

How we used NASA data

Mars Orbiter Laser Altimeter (MOLA) provided elevations for the Gale Crater target. NASADEM provided Earth elevations for the four candidate areas. We derived slope distributions at the same comparison scale and used Wasserstein-1 distance to score the difference.

What the result supports

Areas A and B are candidates for further scouting. This regional run assessed terrain only. It did not assess rover performance, access, or field safety.

The public app offers a separate, labeled practice run where you can plan rover tests and open a modeled field brief.
