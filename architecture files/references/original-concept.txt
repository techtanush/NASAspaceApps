# ANALOGFORGE
## Mission-Conditioned Qualification of Earth Analogs for Moon & Mars Exploration

**Final Research Brief + Hackathon Product Specification**

**One-line idea:** Tell AnalogForge what you need to test on the Moon or Mars and what rover you have. It finds Earth locations that can actually support that test, explains exactly what each site can and cannot reproduce using NASA data, creates rover-specific pass/fail benchmarks, and uses an agentic AI team to design, verify, and re-plan the test.

---

## 1. Challenge

NASA Space Apps 2026 challenge: **Identify Earth Locations that Analog the Permanent Moon Base Locations and Mars.**

NASA asks teams to use open Earth, Moon, and Mars data to identify and characterize terrestrial analogs for future bases/landing sites.

Earth analogs already help mission teams test equipment and methods before flight; the gap is better discovery and characterization.

### Plain-English challenge

Before we send equipment or people to the Moon or Mars, where on Earth can we safely practice—and what exactly does that Earth location realistically simulate?

---

## 2. Core Problem / Insight

The obvious solution is a similarity map.

That will be crowded.

The real insight is:

> **There is no universally best Mars/Moon analog; there is a best analog for a specific experiment.**

A volcanic field can be excellent for mobility/geology and poor for thermal testing.

No Earth location naturally reproduces lunar gravity or vacuum.

AnalogForge therefore asks:

> **What can this site validly test for this mission, what can it not test, and how do we prove that?**

---

# 3. Final Product

## AnalogForge

### Pitch

Tell AnalogForge what you need to test on the Moon or Mars.

It finds the Earth location or locations that can actually support that test, proves what each can and cannot simulate using NASA data, and generates rover-specific benchmarks to verify the test.

### Three Headline Features

1. **Mission Fingerprint**  
   Convert mission intent into measurable test requirements.

2. **Analog Qualification + Analog Gaps**  
   PASS / PARTIAL / FAIL / GAP by requirement.  
   No fake “92% Mars” score.

3. **PROVE IT**  
   Generate a rover-specific field test and execute a deterministic terrain/route check. If the proposed site cannot actually exercise the requirement, reject it or re-plan.

# 5. Mission Fingerprint

AnalogForge should not immediately search Earth.

First, it asks:

> **What does this mission actually need reproduced?**

The Mission Fingerprint transforms the mission objective into measurable test requirements.

Example mission:

> “Test autonomous Mars rover mobility and geological sampling.”

Possible Mission Fingerprint:

### Terrain requirements

- slope distribution
- terrain roughness
- local relief
- traversability

### Science requirements

- exposed geological/mineral features
- sampling access

### Operational requirements

- route length
- energy usage
- navigation requirements

### Environmental requirements

- thermal regime
- aridity
- lighting, if relevant

### Non-Earth-reproducible requirements

- Mars gravity
- lunar gravity
- vacuum
- radiation
- true Martian atmospheric conditions
- exact planetary regolith mechanics

This is a key product principle:

> **AnalogForge should never pretend that an Earth location reproduces a planetary condition that it physically cannot reproduce.**

---

# 6. Analog Qualification

Instead of outputting:

> “This location is 91% Mars.”

AnalogForge outputs an evidence-backed qualification matrix.

Example:

## Candidate Earth Site A

| Mission Requirement | Qualification |
|---|---|
| 8–18° slopes | PASS |
| Rocky terrain | PASS |
| Terrain roughness | PASS |
| Exposed mineral context | PASS |
| Extreme thermal environment | PARTIAL |
| Low-light conditions | FAIL |
| Mars gravity | GAP |
| Mars atmosphere | GAP |

### Qualified For

- rover mobility testing
- autonomous navigation
- geological sampling procedures

### Partially Qualified For

- thermal operations

### Not Qualified For

- atmosphere-dependent testing
- low-gravity mobility behavior
- radiation testing

This is significantly more scientifically useful than a generic similarity percentage.

---

# 7. Analog Gaps

Analog Gaps are a first-class product feature.

For every proposed Earth analog, AnalogForge explicitly states what the Earth site **cannot reproduce**.

Example:

## Analog Coverage

### Reproducible on Earth

- terrain
- slope
- roughness
- geology
- aridity
- some thermal environments
- logistics/isolation

### Partially Reproducible

- illumination
- temperature cycle
- dust characteristics

### Not Naturally Reproducible

- Mars gravity
- lunar gravity
- vacuum
- radiation environment
- planetary atmosphere
- exact regolith mechanics

Instead of hiding these weaknesses, AnalogForge highlights them.

That gives engineers a more honest test plan.

---

# 8. Composite Analog Portfolio

Sometimes one Earth site will not be sufficient.

AnalogForge should be able to conclude:

> **No single Earth location adequately covers this mission test.**

It can then recommend a combination of sites.

Example:

## Mission Requirements

- rough volcanic terrain
- steep slopes
- very cold conditions
- low-light operations

### Site A — Iceland

- geology: strong
- roughness: strong
- slopes: strong
- cold: moderate
- low light: moderate

### Site B — Antarctic region

- geology: moderate
- roughness: moderate
- slopes: moderate
- cold: excellent
- low light: excellent

AnalogForge might conclude:

> **Recommended Test Portfolio: Site A + Site B**

Site A handles:

- mobility
- terrain
- geology

Site B handles:

- cold operations
- low-light operations

Remaining gaps:

- gravity
- vacuum
- radiation

These must be tested through simulation or specialized facilities.

### Key idea

> **Earth is not one Mars simulator. Earth is a distributed planetary test laboratory.**

# 9. Rover-Site Compatibility

Once AnalogForge understands the rover, it compares rover capabilities against the actual Earth test environment.

Example:

| Metric | Rover Capability | Site/Test Requirement | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Maximum slope | 18° | 16° | PASS |
| Ground clearance | 12 cm | 15 cm obstacle | FAIL |
| Battery reserve | 20% minimum | predicted 17% | FAIL |
| Route range | 3 km | 2.4 km | PASS |
| Sensors | LiDAR + stereo | terrain navigation | PASS |

The system can then say:

> **The site is scientifically appropriate, but this rover is not yet ready for the proposed test.**

That is a very important distinction.

The system evaluates:

1. whether the **site** is appropriate;
2. whether the **rover** is capable of executing the required test.

---

# 10. Rover Qualification Benchmarks

AnalogForge should generate concrete benchmarks customized to the rover.

Example rover:

- max safe slope: 18°
- battery: 1.8 kWh
- reserve requirement: 20%
- max route: 3 km
- sensors: stereo + IMU + LiDAR
- objective: geological sampling

AnalogForge might generate:

## Benchmark 1 — Slope Mobility

The rover must traverse:

- 100 m at 8–12°
- 200 m at 12–15°
- 100 m at 15–18°

PASS criteria:

- no immobilization
- never exceed 18°
- no safety violation

---

## Benchmark 2 — Terrain Diversity

The rover must cross at least:

- 3 roughness classes
- 2 relief classes
- one high-complexity terrain segment

PASS criteria:

- reach all required sectors
- preserve navigation accuracy

---

## Benchmark 3 — Energy

Mission must finish with:

> **>= 20% battery reserve**

PASS criteria:

- complete test route
- complete science tasks
- return to base
- battery reserve above threshold

---

## Benchmark 4 — Science Access

Rover must:

- reach 2 geological targets
- stop at each science waypoint
- complete simulated sampling task
- return safely

---

## Benchmark 5 — Recovery

Inject one blocked path.

Rover/test planner must:

- detect blocked segment
- generate alternate path
- preserve science objective
- remain within energy limits

---

# 11. PROVE IT

This should be the main interactive feature.

After AnalogForge qualifies a site, the user clicks:

# PROVE IT

AnalogForge then asks:

> **Can this Earth location actually execute the experiment we claim it can support?**

The system generates a lightweight test using the site's real terrain data.

Example:

### Required Test

- travel 2.2 km
- experience defined slope bands
- cross multiple roughness classes
- reach two geological targets
- never exceed rover's 18° limit
- return with 20% battery reserve

The deterministic engine searches the terrain.

---

## Example Result

### QUALIFICATION FAILED

Reason:

> Candidate Site A contains terrain that statistically matches the target profile, but there is no contiguous test route containing sufficient 12–18° slope exposure.

This result is extremely valuable.

It demonstrates:

> **Similarity does not equal test usefulness.**

AnalogForge then decides what to do next.

Possible actions:

- search another part of the same site;
- alter the route;
- investigate another candidate;
- construct a composite analog portfolio;
- revise the benchmark;
- identify the requirement as impossible to reproduce.

---

# 12. Agent Council

Agentic AI should be central to the project.

However:

> **Agents are operators—not the source of scientific truth.**

NASA/open scientific data and deterministic calculations remain the evidence layer.

One underlying AI model may perform several roles, but conceptually the system should expose a **Mission Council** of specialized agents.

---

## Agent 1 — Mission Director / Orchestrator

Responsible for the overall goal.

It decides:

- what needs to happen next;
- which agent should investigate;
- whether evidence is sufficient;
- whether the workflow should continue;
- whether a result should be rejected.

Example:

> “Terrain evidence is sufficient, but geological evidence is weak. Send the target to the geological analysis tool before qualification.”

---

## Agent 2 — Requirements Agent

Converts mission language into a structured Mission Test Contract.

Input:

> “Test autonomous rover navigation on steep rocky Mars terrain while collecting samples.”

Output:

- mobility requirement
- terrain requirements
- science requirements
- rover constraints
- environmental dimensions
- success criteria

---

## Agent 3 — Rover Systems Agent

Reads the rover profile.

Determines:

- hard limits
- safety thresholds
- battery constraints
- mobility constraints
- sensor capabilities
- achievable mission objectives

Then generates rover-specific benchmarks.

---

## Agent 4 — Planetary Target Agent

Characterizes the Moon/Mars target region using approved scientific tools.

It may request:

- target elevation
- slope
- roughness
- relief
- thermal information
- mineral context

The agent itself does not calculate these values.

It calls deterministic tools that do.

---

## Agent 5 — Earth Analog Scout

Searches Earth candidate regions.

Uses a two-stage approach:

### Stage 1

Coarse screening.

### Stage 2

Detailed investigation of top candidates.

This avoids expensive full-resolution global processing.

---

## Agent 6 — Qualification Agent

Builds the qualification matrix.

For each requirement:

- PASS
- PARTIAL
- FAIL
- GAP

Then determines what the Earth location can legitimately test.

---

## Agent 7 — Scientific Critic

This is one of the most important agents.

Its job is to attack the recommendation.

Questions include:

- Is this comparison scientifically valid?
- Are spatial resolutions comparable?
- Is this a direct measurement or a weak proxy?
- Is evidence missing?
- Did we overstate similarity?
- Is there another candidate that could overturn the conclusion?
- Are we trying to reproduce something Earth cannot actually reproduce?

The critic can send the workflow backward.

Example:

> “Candidate A's terrain match is strong, but mineral evidence is unavailable. Qualification confidence is insufficient. Investigate Candidate B or retrieve another geological source.”

---

## Agent 8 — Test Designer

Takes the qualified site and rover.

Creates the smallest useful experiment that can verify the qualification.

Outputs:

- route objective
- slope exposures
- roughness exposures
- science targets
- energy constraints
- success criteria

---

## Agent 9 — Verification & Replanning Agent

Reads the output of the deterministic test engine.

Possible outcomes:

### PASS

Qualification supported.

### FAIL — route problem

Generate another route.

### FAIL — rover problem

Modify test or flag design limitation.

### FAIL — analog problem

Reject site and return to Earth Analog Scout.

### FAIL — requirement impossible

Move requirement into Analog Gap.

This agent creates the visible:

> plan → test → inspect → diagnose → re-plan

loop.

---

## Agent 10 — Evidence / Analog Passport Agent

Produces the final report.

Every claim must link back to:

- NASA dataset
- derived calculation
- mission requirement
- qualification decision
- verification result

The report should not be a generic LLM summary.

It should be evidence-backed.

---

# 13. Agent Committee Behavior

Do not create agents only because “multi-agent AI” sounds impressive.

The important part is that they **disagree and verify**.

Example:

### Scout Agent

> “Candidate Iceland-17 strongly matches the requested terrain.”

### Qualification Agent

> “Terrain PASS, geology PARTIAL, thermal FAIL.”

### Scientific Critic

> “The geology conclusion uses a weak proxy. Confidence too low.”

### Mission Director

> “Retrieve additional geological evidence.”

### Data tool

Processes another dataset.

### Qualification Agent

Updates result.

### Test Designer

Creates field benchmark.

### Verification Agent

Runs test.

### Result

FAIL.

### Scientific Critic

> “Failure indicates insufficient contiguous slope exposure, not rover limitation.”

### Mission Director

Reopens Earth search.

That is agentic AI that visibly performs work.

The system needs clear separation between:

## Agent Decisions

Agents:

- decide questions;
- choose tools;
- select investigations;
- interpret evidence;
- decide what to investigate next;
- design tests;
- diagnose failures.

Code performs:

- raster processing
- slope calculation
- terrain roughness
- local relief
- coordinate transforms
- feature comparison
- route search
- coverage calculations
- simple energy estimation

---

## Optimization

Algorithms perform:

- candidate ranking
- composite-site selection
- route optimization
- minimal repair
- test coverage optimization

---

## Verification

Produces machine-readable:

- PASS
- PARTIAL
- FAIL
- GAP

Required for:

- changing important assumptions;
- real-world physical testing;

### Non-negotiable rule

> **NASA/open scientific data + deterministic calculations are the truth layer. Agents decide what to do next.**

# 24. Failure Diagnosis

The agent must determine **why** the test failed.

Possible causes:

### Route Problem

A different path may work.

### Rover Problem

Rover specification cannot meet the mission.

### Analog Problem

Site cannot reproduce the required condition.

### Data Problem

Evidence is insufficient.

### Requirement Problem

Requirement cannot be naturally reproduced on Earth.

The AI should not simply say:

> “Test failed.”

It should identify the failure category.

---

# 25. Replanning

Example:

> “Failure was caused by insufficient terrain exposure, not rover capability.”

AnalogForge then:

1. searches a different section of the site;
2. runs route optimizer again;
3. evaluates test;
4. if still insufficient, reopens candidate search.

It may conclude:

> Candidate A cannot fully validate requirement R3.

Then:

> Investigating Candidate B.

Or:

> Adding Candidate B to the test portfolio.

That is the strongest agentic moment in the demo.

# 33. Why Agentic AI Is Necessary

A judge may ask:

> “Couldn't this just be a normal algorithm?”

The answer:

A static algorithm can rank predefined environmental features.

But AnalogForge must handle **different mission questions**.

For one rover mission, terrain roughness might matter most.

For another mission, thermal exposure matters.

For geological sampling, mineral context becomes critical.

The agentic layer:

1. understands the mission;
2. identifies required evidence;
3. selects the right scientific tools;
4. executes analyses;
5. inspects results;
6. notices missing evidence;
7. asks the critic to challenge conclusions;
8. designs the verification experiment;
9. reads test results;
10. determines why failure occurred;
11. re-plans.

That open-ended scientific workflow is the reason agents matter.

---

# 34. Agentic AI Principle

The AI should never say:

> “I think this location is 89% similar.”

Instead it should say:

> “The mission requires terrain roughness evidence. I am invoking the terrain-analysis tool.”

Then the tool produces the number.

The agent decides **what question to ask next**.

The deterministic engine produces **the answer**.

# 35. Validation

This is critical for global-level credibility.

## Validation Test 1 — Known Analog Recovery

Use known NASA analog sites.

For the appropriate mission objective, AnalogForge should recognize why those sites are useful.

---

## Validation Test 2 — Negative Control

Use a clearly poor candidate.

Example:

A tropical low-relief environment should not qualify for a Mars rocky-mobility test because one isolated statistic happens to match.

---

## Validation Test 3 — Mission Sensitivity

Use the same planetary target.

Change the mission objective.

Example:

### Mission A

Rover mobility.

### Mission B

Thermal operations.

If AnalogForge returns exactly the same qualification/ranking, the system is probably not truly mission-conditioned.

---

## Validation Test 4 — PROVE IT

A statistically promising site should sometimes fail actual benchmark execution.

That proves the system is doing more than similarity matching.

# 38. Product Philosophy

AnalogForge should feel like:

> **a miniature AI-native planetary mission test engineering environment**

not:

> a NASA-themed chatbot.

The system should visually demonstrate that the AI:

- plans;
- investigates;
- calls tools;
- checks evidence;
- gets challenged;
- runs a test;
- learns from failure;
- re-plans.

---

# 39. Important Scientific Language

Avoid phrases such as:

> “Site X is exactly like Mars.”

> “Site Y is 95% Mars.”

> “This perfectly simulates lunar conditions.”

Instead say:

> “Qualified for this mission test.”

> “Strong terrain analog.”

> “Partial thermal analog.”

> “This condition cannot be reproduced by site selection alone.”

> “Additional simulation/facility testing required.”

This language makes the product much more credible.

---

# 40. Non-Goals

AnalogForge must **not** claim:

- NASA does not already use terrestrial analogs.
- NASA lacks planetary field-testing expertise.
- AI replaces NASA scientists.
- Earth can reproduce every planetary condition.
- similarity score equals mission readiness.
- generated tests are mission-certified.
- a hackathon prototype can validate flight hardware.

Instead:

> AnalogForge helps make analog-selection reasoning explicit, mission-specific, evidence-backed, and computationally testable.

# 42. Final Scope Lock

## Build This

AnalogForge is an **agentic, mission-conditioned Earth analog qualification tool**.

It takes:

**Moon/Mars mission objective**

+

**rover profile**

and uses NASA planetary and Earth data to:

1. understand what the mission needs tested;
2. characterize the target planetary environment;
3. discover candidate Earth sites;
4. qualify each site requirement-by-requirement;
5. explicitly identify Analog Gaps;
6. compare rover capabilities to test requirements;
7. generate rover-specific pass/fail benchmarks;
8. design a lightweight field test;
9. execute a deterministic PROVE IT analysis;
10. diagnose failures;
11. re-plan when necessary;
12. produce an evidence-backed Analog Passport.

# 43. Local Hackathon Success Definition

We do **not** need ten scenarios.

We need one excellent scenario.

Success means:

- real NASA terrain data;
- real mission-specific qualification;
- real rover constraints;
- multiple collaborating agent roles;
- one Scientific Critic loop;
- one rover benchmark;
- one actual computed failure;
- one genuine agent re-plan;
- one polished Analog Passport.

If those pieces work together smoothly, the project will feel significantly more complete than a normal analog map.
